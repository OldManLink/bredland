# 🔐 BRD-038 — Script manual Bredland TLS certificate renewal

## Status

Planned

## Goal

Create a safe, repeatable, manually initiated procedure for renewing the TLS certificate used by Bredland.

The renewal shall be driven explicitly by the operator from the Mac.

Scripts shall automate the mechanical and error-prone parts of the procedure while preserving human control over when renewal occurs.

This slice does **not** introduce unattended certificate renewal.

---

## Background

BRD-034 added monitoring of Bredland's TLS certificate expiry.

The NOC now reports the certificate expiry as an absolute timestamp and can warn before the certificate reaches its deadline.

That solves detection:

```text
certificate approaching expiry
        ↓
NOC notification
```

It does not yet solve the operational response.

The current certificate requires a manual renewal workflow involving Bredland and the DNS infrastructure used to satisfy the ACME challenge.

The next step is therefore to make that manual operation reproducible:

```text
NOC warns
    ↓
operator chooses to renew
    ↓
Mac runs renewal tooling
    ↓
ACME challenge is completed
    ↓
certificate is renewed
    ↓
Bredland serves renewed certificate
    ↓
telemetry reports new expiry
    ↓
NOC notification clears naturally
```

---

## Principle

> Automate the procedure, not the decision.

Certificate renewal is infrequent, security-sensitive operational work.

The immediate goal is not zero-touch automation.

The goal is to replace an improvised sequence of commands with a checked, repeatable procedure that can be run confidently when the certificate approaches expiry.

The scripts should therefore favour:

- explicit operator initiation;
- visible progress;
- validation before destructive or externally visible steps;
- immediate failure on unexpected conditions;
- clear verification of the resulting certificate;
- repeatability;
- documentation close to the executable procedure.

---

## Execution Model

The renewal procedure shall be initiated manually from the operator's Mac.

Conceptually:

```text
Mac
 │
 │ explicit operator command
 ▼
renewal tooling
 │
 ├── Bredland
 │
 └── DNS / ACME challenge infrastructure
        │
        ▼
   certificate authority
```

The Mac is the orchestration point.

Bredland shall not gain an unattended certificate-renewal scheduler as part of this slice.

---

## Existing Monitoring Contract

BRD-034 remains responsible for observing certificate expiry.

BRD-038 shall not duplicate that monitoring logic.

After successful renewal, Bredland's existing telemetry shall report the new certificate expiry timestamp.

The existing NOC Rule shall then naturally stop producing the expiry warning once the renewed certificate is observed.

No explicit "clear certificate warning" action is required.

---

## Investigation

Before implementing the final scripts, rehearse and document the exact renewal path required by the current certificate and DNS setup.

Confirm:

- how the current certificate was issued;
- the ACME client and invocation required for renewal;
- the DNS challenge mechanism;
- how the required `_acme-challenge` record is created;
- how DNS propagation is verified;
- how the ACME client continues after challenge creation;
- where the renewed certificate and private key are written;
- how Bredland begins serving the renewed certificate;
- whether the serving process requires reload or restart;
- how the served certificate is independently verified from the Mac;
- how temporary DNS challenge state is cleaned up.

Do not encode assumptions about these steps before they have been exercised against the real environment.

---

## Script Structure

The exact number and division of scripts shall emerge from the real renewal procedure.

Do not create a framework merely because the operation contains several steps.

A single script, several small scripts, or a thin orchestration script over existing commands are all acceptable if they make the procedure clearer and safer.

The resulting command structure should make the operator's progress obvious.

For example, the workflow may naturally contain phases resembling:

```text
preflight
    ↓
request / prepare challenge
    ↓
publish DNS challenge
    ↓
verify challenge visibility
    ↓
complete renewal
    ↓
activate certificate
    ↓
verify served certificate
    ↓
cleanup
```

This is a workflow sketch, not a prescribed implementation.

---

## Preflight Checks

Before attempting renewal, the tooling should verify the prerequisites that can be checked safely.

Candidates include:

- Bredland is reachable;
- required local tools exist;
- required credentials/configuration are available;
- the current certificate can be inspected;
- its current expiry can be displayed;
- the expected hostname/certificate identity is being operated on;
- required remote endpoints are reachable.

Preflight failure shall stop the procedure before certificate state is changed.

---

## DNS Challenge

The DNS challenge is a security-sensitive boundary.

The scripts shall automate only the specific DNS operation required for Bredland certificate renewal.

Credentials shall not be committed to the repository.

The procedure shall avoid broadening credential authority merely to make scripting easier.

If the current DNS provider cannot provide suitably restricted credentials for safe unattended operation, that is acceptable.

BRD-038 is explicitly a manually initiated workflow.

The operator may authenticate interactively where that provides the safer boundary.

---

## Secrets

No certificate private keys, passwords, API tokens, SSH credentials, ACME account secrets or other operational secrets shall be committed to the repository.

Scripts may consume credentials from appropriate existing local mechanisms or explicitly documented ignored configuration.

Failure messages shall not print secrets.

Test fixtures shall use synthetic values.

---

## Certificate Activation

Successful issuance alone is not sufficient.

The renewed certificate must become the certificate actually served by Bredland.

The procedure shall make this transition explicit and verify it afterwards.

If a service reload or restart is required, perform only the minimum operation necessary.

Failure during activation shall be reported clearly.

Do not silently report renewal success merely because certificate files were created.

---

## Verification

The procedure shall independently verify the result after activation.

At minimum, verify the certificate actually served by Bredland rather than merely inspecting a newly written local file.

Verification should establish:

- the expected certificate identity;
- the new expiry timestamp;
- that the expiry is later than the certificate being replaced;
- that the trusted Bredland HTTPS endpoint is serving the renewed certificate.

Where practical, display the old and new expiry values clearly to the operator.

The renewal is not complete until the served certificate has been verified.

---

## Failure Behaviour

Failure shall be explicit.

The scripts shall:

- stop on failed prerequisites;
- avoid continuing blindly after an unsuccessful ACME step;
- distinguish issuance failure from activation failure;
- avoid reporting success until the served certificate is verified;
- leave enough diagnostic information for the operator to understand which phase failed;
- avoid exposing secrets in diagnostics.

Where temporary state has been created, cleanup behaviour shall be deliberate.

Do not sacrifice useful failure evidence merely to make cleanup automatic.

---

## Idempotence and Repeatability

The procedure should be safe to rehearse and repeat where the underlying tools permit it.

Do not depend on undocumented shell state or commands remembered from the previous renewal.

The repository shall contain enough documentation that the next renewal can begin from:

```text
NOC says certificate is approaching expiry
```

without reconstructing the procedure from chat history.

### 1. Reconstruct the real renewal path

### 2. Rehearse the sensitive boundaries

Confirm independently:

- Bredland access;
- certificate inspection;
- ACME invocation;
- DNS challenge creation;
- DNS challenge verification;
- certificate output;
- activation mechanism;
- served-certificate verification.

Prefer harmless inspection commands before changing live state.

---

### 3. Establish script test infrastructure

Identify the shell/script testing style already used by the repository.

Add deterministic tests around the orchestration logic where practical.

External systems shall be represented by controlled test doubles/fixtures rather than contacted by the automated test suite.

---

### 4. Implement preflight

Drive the checks needed to prevent the most obvious unsafe or accidental execution.

Verify that preflight failure stops before renewal begins.

---

### 5. Script the renewal workflow incrementally

Automate one demonstrated manual step at a time.

Keep external commands visible and explicit.

Avoid hiding operational behaviour behind unnecessary abstraction.

---

### 6. Add activation

Make installation/activation of the renewed certificate an explicit phase.

Test failure handling around this boundary.

---

### 7. Add independent post-renewal verification

Verify the certificate actually served by Bredland.

The script shall not declare success before this verification passes.

---

### 8. Document operator usage

Document:

- prerequisites;
- how to start renewal;
- expected interactive steps;
- expected output;
- success criteria;
- likely failure points;
- how to inspect the current certificate manually;
- how to recover or investigate after a failed run.

---

### 9. Perform the real renewal

When renewal is appropriate, execute the scripted procedure against the production Bredland certificate.

Observe every phase.

Do not treat the first production execution as unattended.

---

### 10. Verify NOC convergence

After renewal:

- confirm Bredland serves the new certificate;
- confirm its new expiry independently;
- wait for normal Bredland telemetry;
- verify `tls_cert_expires_at` reports the new deadline;
- verify the existing expiry notification disappears naturally.

## Out of Scope

- Fully unattended certificate renewal.
- Scheduled certificate renewal.
- Automatic renewal triggered by NOC telemetry.
- A trusted-dashboard "renew certificate" action.
- General certificate-management infrastructure.
- General DNS automation.
- Changing DNS provider.
- Broadening DNS credentials for convenience.
- Storing secrets in the repository.
- Replacing BRD-034 monitoring.
- Changing the certificate-expiry Rule.
- Inventing a generic remote-operation framework.
- Automatic recovery from arbitrary renewal failures.

## Notes

BRD-034 answered:

> How do we know the certificate needs attention?

BRD-038 answers:

> Once we know, how do we renew it safely and repeatably?

Keeping those concerns separate is deliberate.

The intended operational model is:

```text
monitor automatically
        ↓
warn automatically
        ↓
decide manually
        ↓
renew with scripted assistance
        ↓
verify automatically where practical
        ↓
telemetry observes reality
```

This gives us most of the reliability benefit of automation without prematurely granting unattended infrastructure the authority required to alter DNS and certificates.

A future slice may revisit unattended renewal if experience with several manual renewals demonstrates a sufficiently narrow and trustworthy authorization path.

Until then, a boring command run deliberately from the Mac is a feature, not a limitation.
